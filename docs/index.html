Escriba

Transcribes your Google Meet calls to Markdown, with the name of who is speaking. No bot joins the call and nothing leaves your computer.

Install Source on GitHub

How it works

  1. Join a meeting. Escriba turns on Meet's captions and hides them, so the video keeps its full size. The icon shows REC.
  2. Talk as usual. It reads each caption as Meet writes it, with the speaker's name. No audio is recorded.
  3. Leave the call. The transcript is saved as a Markdown file in your Downloads folder and on a transcripts page you can search.

Ask your AI about your meetings

An optional MCP server, installed apart from the extension, lets Claude, Cursor or any other app that supports MCP list, search and read your transcripts. Ask "What did we agree in today's product sync?" and get the decisions, with who said what and when. With Claude Code: 

claude mcp add escriba -- npx -y escriba-mcp

The server runs on your computer, only reads the transcript files and makes no network requests. The AI app you connect it to does receive what it reads: a cloud assistant sends that text to its provider. Setup for Claude Desktop and other apps.

What it looks like

Privacy

  • No network requests, analytics, accounts or remote code. This page loads nothing from other sites either.
  • No audio or video is recorded, and the meeting chat is not read.
  • Transcripts stay in your Downloads folder and in the browser's local storage.
  • The optional MCP server only reads those files. If you connect it to an AI app, that app receives what it reads.
  • Other people in the call should know you are transcribing it.

Read the privacy policy

Good to know

  • Meet transcribes one language at a time, the one chosen in its caption settings. Escriba shows which one is active.
  • Only what is said after you join is captured.
  • It depends on Meet's page, which Google changes. If it stops transcribing, the icon turns amber and you get a notification.
  • Works in Chrome, Edge and Brave. The interface is in English and Spanish.
